# Resume bullets

Each set uses only results computed in this project. The confirmatory coefficient is an association, not a causal elasticity, and no price change was deployed.

## Economic consulting analyst

- Estimated a pre-specified log-log association between ready-to-eat cereal movement and unit price on 4,707,776 Dominick's UPC-store-weeks from the Kilts Center files, with UPC-by-store and week fixed effects and store-clustered standard errors. The confirmatory coefficient was −2.128 (SE 0.026).
- Audited ten proposed instruments, including margin-derived cost, lagged prices, and other-store prices, rejected all of them, and did not estimate two-stage least squares on the scanner data.
- Wrote a research paper and a two-page pricing memo showing that, if the confirmatory association is used only as a hypothetical elasticity, revenue and accounting gross profit move in opposite directions. Recommended a designed price test rather than a rollout.

## Research analyst, economics

- Built an analysis panel from 6,602,582 Dominick's cereal movement rows, retained recorded zeros instead of filling missing weeks, and estimated the confirmatory specification on the 4,707,776 positive-sale rows (93 stores, 489 UPCs, 366 weeks).
- Compared pooled OLS (−0.346) with UPC, store, pair, and week-effect specifications. The pre-specified pair-and-week association was −2.128 and was not selected by searching over R².
- Separated sampling uncertainty from specification uncertainty. The store-clustered interval for the confirmatory slope is about [−2.18, −2.08]. The pooled-versus-fixed-effects gap does change the sign of a revenue calculation. A synthetic instrumental-variables example, with no Kilts rows, recovers a known slope only when the exclusion restriction holds.

## Pricing and revenue optimization analyst

- Quantified historical price support for Dominick's cereals: a 10 percent increase from the last observed price lies outside the pair's own price range for about 68 percent of UPC-store pairs.
- Under the confirmatory association used only as a hypothetical elasticity, a 10 percent price increase on the pairs whose history contains the whole menu lowers revenue by about 10 percent and raises accounting gross profit by about 16 percent. Accounting gross profit uses average acquisition cost, not marginal cost.
- Documented that both revenue and accounting gross profit rise together only under the pooled association of −0.346, which the identification audit rejected as a causal elasticity. No scenario was marked supported for deployment.
